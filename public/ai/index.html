What is RedSecAI?

RedSecAI is a local or Ollama-backed assistant for RedSecTools. It can answer general questions, inspect scoped RedSecTools data through approved tools, and prepare platform actions for you to confirm. It runs under your logged-in session, so role permissions still apply.

What it can do

  • Calendar and projects - read permitted schedules, find projects, prepare entries, allocations, reminders, leave, public holidays, and project changes.
  • Wiki - search pages, read page content, draft improvements, and prepare confirmation-gated page updates.
  • Threat intelligence - summarize alerts, feeds, keywords, tags, MITRE mappings, IOCs, and feed health from RedSecThreat data.
  • Reporter and surveys - help with permitted project metadata, findings, notes, comments, report sections, templates, survey metadata, and results.
  • Homepage operations - prepare permitted bulletin, shortcut, and tool-favourite changes.

What it cannot do

  • No encrypted plaintext - it cannot read decrypted RedSecPaste, RedSecShare, RedSecTeam, or RedSecVault content.
  • No secrets access - it cannot see vault passwords, API keys, SSH keys, TOTP secrets, recovery codes, or browser-held encryption keys.
  • No RBAC bypass - it cannot perform actions your account is not allowed to perform.
  • No arbitrary backend access - it can only use the scoped AI tools exposed by RedSecTools.
  • No silent writes - create, update, delete, assign, publish, and scheduling actions require a confirmation card.

How tool use works

1. Decide

The model decides whether it needs RedSecTools data or can answer directly.

2. Inspect

Read-only tools can fetch scoped data, such as calendar state, wiki matches, or threat alerts.

3. Confirm

Write tools create an action card. The action is not applied until you confirm it.

Prompting for best results

  • Ask for one action at a time.
    Find the target, prepare one change, confirm it, then ask for the next step.
  • Refresh for unrelated work.
    Use the refresh control when switching from one topic or workflow to another.
  • Name the target when you know it.
    Project names, wiki titles, alert tags, dates, and user names reduce ambiguity.
  • Be explicit with dates.
    Say the year for backdated work or future months that could be ambiguous.
  • Ask it to check first when unsure.
    For example, ask it to find matching projects or wiki pages before preparing changes.
  • Verify important writes.
    After confirming an action card, ask it to check the created or updated record.

Example prompts

Check my calendar for tomorrow in Australia/Sydney and list scheduled entries with times.
Search the wiki for pages mentioning password reset and summarize the matching page titles before editing anything.
Find RedSecThreat alerts tagged Fortinet from the last 7 days and summarize severity, source, and IOCs.
Prepare one bulletin that says "Maintenance window tonight", style it as a notice, and expire it at 9 pm today.
Find the project named "External retest" and show its current schedule before preparing any allocation.
After I confirm the action card, check the record and tell me whether the change actually applied.
