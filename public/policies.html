Privacy Policy

This Privacy Policy explains how personal information and workspace data may be handled when you use this RedSecTools deployment. RedSecTools is designed for self-hosted security operations, so the organisation or person operating this instance is responsible for how the service is configured, administered, backed up, monitored, and used.

1. Who Is Responsible

The operator of this RedSecTools instance is the controller or responsible party for user accounts, workspace data, retention settings, access decisions, audit review, backups, integrations, and support processes. Users should contact their workspace administrator for privacy requests, account questions, retention requests, or security concerns related to this deployment.

2. Information Processed

Depending on how the workspace is used, RedSecTools may process account details such as username, email address, password hash, role and permission assignments, MFA state, trusted device records, invitation status, profile settings, notification preferences, and authentication events.

The platform may also process workspace data such as paste and file-share metadata, survey content and responses, wiki pages, calendar entries, threat feeds, reports, proposals, engagement records, MiniTool lookup inputs and results, integration settings, webhook delivery metadata, service-account metadata, and administrative configuration.

3. Encrypted Content

RedSecTools is designed so plaintext content is encrypted in the browser before being submitted to the server. During normal operation, the server stores opaque ciphertext and does not receive paste, file, chat, or vault plaintext. This does not protect against a malicious or compromised server that serves modified JavaScript to clients.

Encrypted content may still have associated metadata, such as ownership, timestamps, expiry settings, access state, file names where provided, size, route identifiers, and audit events. Workspace administrators may be able to view or export metadata and encrypted records depending on their permissions.

4. Logs, Audit Events, and Security Records

RedSecTools records security and operational events to support account protection, incident investigation, compliance review, and system administration. These records may include IP-derived request metadata, user agent information, timestamps, account identifiers, route or action names, success or failure state, administrative changes, API-token activity, webhook activity, SSO events, and security-sensitive workflow events.

The application is designed not to log plaintext passwords, recovery codes, TOTP secrets, API tokens, webhook secrets, encryption keys, URL fragment keys, decrypted paste/share/chat/vault content, or other secrets. Operators should preserve that model when adding integrations, proxies, monitoring, or custom logging.

5. Cookies and Local Storage

RedSecTools uses signed, HTTP-only cookies for session management and may use browser storage for theme preferences, client-side keys, encrypted-workflow state, and user-interface preferences. Disabling cookies or clearing local browser storage may prevent login, MFA trust, encrypted content access, chat key recovery, or other application features from working correctly.

6. External Services and Integrations

Some features may contact third-party or external services if an administrator enables or configures them. Examples may include SMTP providers, SAML identity providers, AI model providers, webhook destinations, threat-intelligence sources, MiniTool API providers, DNS/security lookup services, and browser-accessed public APIs. Data sent to those services is governed by the operator's configuration and the relevant provider's terms and privacy practices.

RedSecAI and API-backed MiniTools should be used deliberately. Do not submit secrets, regulated data, client confidential data, or personal information to external AI or third-party APIs unless your organisation has approved that use.

7. Data Retention and Deletion

Retention depends on workspace configuration, feature-specific expiry settings, backups, audit requirements, and administrator actions. Some content can expire automatically, be burned after access, or be deleted by authorised users. Audit logs, backups, and security records may be retained separately for operational, legal, or security reasons.

8. Access and Administration

Workspace administrators can manage users, roles, permissions, MFA state, security settings, integrations, audit exports, backups, branding, and other deployment controls. Administrators should grant access only to authorised personnel and periodically review roles, service accounts, webhook endpoints, SSO settings, external API keys, and backup handling.

9. Security

RedSecTools includes security controls such as authentication, role-based permissions, MFA support, signed cookies, CSP-oriented frontend patterns, audit logging, rate limits, and encrypted workflows for protected content. No system can guarantee absolute security. Users must protect their devices, sessions, MFA factors, recovery codes, browser profiles, downloaded files, exported reports, and any copied secrets or decrypted content.

10. User Rights and Requests

Depending on applicable law and the operator's obligations, users may have rights to request access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability, or objection relating to personal information. Requests should be directed to the workspace administrator or organisation operating this deployment.

11. Children

RedSecTools is intended for professional security and operational use. It is not intended for children or for use by anyone who is not authorised by the workspace operator.

12. Changes

The operator may update this Privacy Policy to reflect legal, operational, security, or product changes. Continued use of the workspace after an update means the updated policy applies from its effective date.
